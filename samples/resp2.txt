Gravity is a natural phenomenon that describes the attraction between objects that have mass. 
Isaac Newton formulated the first mathematical description of gravity, showing that every object 
with mass attracts every other object. The force increases with mass and decreases with distance.

Albert Einstein later refined our understanding through general relativity, describing gravity 
not as a force but as a curvature in the fabric of spacetime. Massive objects bend spacetime, 
and other objects follow those curves — which we experience as gravitational pull.

Gravity is responsible for keeping Earth's atmosphere, holding the Moon in orbit, and 
governing the structure of galaxies. It is considered the weakest fundamental force, yet 
it operates over infinite distances.
